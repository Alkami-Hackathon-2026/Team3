### What Is This and Why Does It Matter?

Before you write a line of code, you need to submit an SDK Project Proposal. This is submitted as a Jira ticket and is the starting point for every SDK project — no exceptions.

The proposal exists because building on the Alkami SDK means building inside a live financial platform. What seems straightforward on the surface can involve authentication requirements, data contracts, third-party dependencies, or platform constraints that aren't obvious until someone familiar with the platform takes a look. The earlier we know what you're building, the earlier we can catch those issues — before they become blockers mid-development.

When you submit a proposal, the Alkami SDK team reviews it from two angles:

- Product review — Does this make sense as a feature on the Alkami platform? Does it fit within the intended use of the SDK?
- Technology review — Is this technically feasible as proposed? Are there platform constraints, dependencies, or integration patterns we need to address before you build?

Depending on what you're building, we may route your proposal to other internal Alkami teams to assess feasibility before giving you a green light.

⚠️ Important: Incomplete Submissions Will Delay Your Review

We review proposals in the order they come in. If your submission is missing information from any of the sections below, we will put your review on hold until the gaps are filled. The fastest path to approval is a complete submission up front.

⚠️ A Note on Complex Integrations

Not every project can be approved for standard SDK development. Integrations that involve deep third-party dependencies, non-standard authentication flows, or data patterns outside the platform's design may require a consultation with Alkami's Professional Services (PSO) team before work can proceed. PSO engagements are scoped and priced separately. If your project falls into this category, we'll tell you after reviewing your proposal — but submitting a proposal does not guarantee approval to build.

### How to Submit

Submit your proposal as a Jira ticket using the Feature Request issue type. In the description field, address each of the sections below. You do not need to use a specific format — clear, plain language is fine. Attach any supporting documents (mockups, diagrams, vendor documentation) directly to the ticket.

When creating the ticket, set the following fields exactly as shown:

| Field | Value |
| Label | sdk_project_proposal |
| Team | SDK |
| Component | SDK |

Note: The Team field is not available on the Jira create screen. After you create the ticket, edit it and set Team to SDK.

Your submission must address every section. If something genuinely doesn't apply to your project, say so explicitly — don't leave it blank.

### Section 1: About Your Project

Tell us what you're building and why. This section should give someone who has never seen your banking app a clear picture of the feature and the problem it solves.

Your submission must include:

- The name of the project
- A plain-language description of what you're building — what does it do, what does the user see and interact with?
- What problem this solves for your members or users
- Whether this is a net-new build, an enhancement to something already in your environment, or a replacement of an existing integration
### Section 2: Who's Involved

We need to know who is doing the work and whether any outside parties are under contract. Projects involving system integrators, vendors, or third-party partners carry additional complexity, and contracted deadlines affect how we prioritize our review.

Your submission must include:

- The name, email, and organization of the primary developer contact
- Whether this is being built in-house, by a third-party partner, an SI, or a combination — and if outside parties are involved, their company name and contact information
- Whether there is a contract between your organization and a third-party vendor, partner, or SI for this project, and if so, whether that contract includes a delivery deadline
- Whether any Alkami team (Support, PSO, Sales, etc.) has already been involved in scoping or discussing this project
### Section 3: User Experience

We need to understand what the experience actually looks like before we can evaluate whether it's feasible. Rough mockups are better than nothing. A written description of the flow is required even if you don't have visuals yet.

Your submission must include:

- Who the intended users are — end users in the banking app, admins in the admin portal, or both
- A step-by-step walkthrough of the user journey: what does the user do, what do they see, what is the outcome?
- Any mockups, wireframes, or screenshots of the expected experience, attached to the ticket if available
- Whether access to this feature will be gated by user permissions, account type, or Alkami package — and if so, how
### Section 4: Architecture & Technical Approach

This is the most critical section for the technology review. You don't need to have every detail figured out — but we need enough to understand the shape of what you're building and identify any platform constraints early.

Your submission must include:

- The type of build: widget, microservice, SSO integration, admin widget, or a combination
- A description of your proposed architecture — how are the pieces connected? What does the data flow look like at a high level?
- Which Alkami services, microservices, or data sources you plan to use (list what you know; we'll help fill gaps)
- Whether this project will connect to anything outside the Alkami platform — a vendor API, a core banking system, or any service hosted outside of Alkami. If yes: what service, what data moves between systems, and whether you have API documentation or technical specs from that vendor
- Whether this project involves any financial transactions — transfers, payments, remittances, or similar
- Whether there are any credentials, certificates, or configurations that Alkami would need to set up or allow for this to work
### Section 5: Data & Security

Any project that touches user or financial data gets additional scrutiny. Be specific here — vague answers will result in follow-up questions that slow your review.

Your submission must include:

- What user or financial data this project will access, transmit, or store
- Whether any sensitive data (PII, account numbers, credentials, financial data) will leave the Alkami platform — and if so, where it goes, how it is secured, and how long it is retained
### Section 6: Timeline & Milestones

We use timeline information to prioritize reviews and flag risks early. If there is a hard deadline tied to a contract, we need to know about it at submission — not after your review is underway.

Your submission must include:

- Your target go-live date
- Any key internal milestones or checkpoints you've already defined
- Whether a contract between your organization and a third party includes a delivery deadline for this project — and if so, who the parties are and what the date is
- Where you are in the process today: not started, in planning, or already in development

⚠️ If You've Already Started Development

Starting development before receiving explicit approval from Alkami is a risk you are taking on yourself. If your project runs into platform constraints, compatibility issues, or requires significant rework after the fact, Alkami's ability to support you will be limited — and we won't be able to prioritize issues that stem from unapproved work. This is not a penalty; it's the reality of building inside a shared platform without a prior review. If you've already started, disclose it in your submission. We'll still review the project, but the level of support Alkami can provide going forward will reflect the circumstances.

### Section 7: Supporting Materials

Attach anything that helps us understand what you're building. The more context we have, the faster and more accurate our review will be.

Useful attachments include:

- Mockups or wireframes
- User journey or flow diagram
- Architecture diagram
- Data flow diagram (required if your project connects to anything outside the Alkami platform)
- Vendor or third-party API documentation
- Vendor contract or SOW (redacted is fine — we just need to understand the scope and any deadline commitments)
### What Happens After You Submit

Once your Jira ticket is submitted, the Alkami SDK team will review it and respond within 5 business days. Depending on what you've submitted, we may:

- Approve the project for standard SDK development and connect you with onboarding resources
- Ask follow-up questions before making a determination
- Route your proposal to additional internal teams for a feasibility assessment
- Flag the project for PSO consultation if it exceeds standard SDK scope

Submitting a proposal does not constitute approval to begin development. If you've already started, note that in Section 6.
